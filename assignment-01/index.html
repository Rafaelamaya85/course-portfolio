My Top 5 Favorite Characters from The Lord of the Rings

One of my favorite things about The Lord of the Rings is that the greatest characters aren't always the strongest or most powerful. Sometimes they're just ordinary people who show courage, loyalty, kindness, and a willingness to do what's right. This is my top 5 favorite characters from the books. Each of these characters are very different, but each one has qualities I admire and lessons to learn from.

  1. Samwise Gamgee

    Samwise Gamgee is, in my opinion, the heart of The Lord of the Rings and its unsung hero. What I admire most about Sam is his unwavering loyalty to Frodo. He puts his entire life on hold to help his friend on a journey he doesn't even fully understand. He doesn't care about fame, power, or getting credit for anything. He just loves his friends, loves the Shire, and wants to do the right thing. I think we could all stand to be a little more like Sam. He reminds me that you don't have to come from a long line of kings, have incredible powers, or even be someone important to make a difference. In Tolkien's Middle-earth, it was a simple gardener who helped change the course of history.

  2. Gandalf

    Gandalf is one of the most powerful beings in Middle-earth, but what I admire most about him has little to do with power. It's his wisdom, patience, and the way he treats others. Unlike Saruman, Gandalf never wanted to use his power to rule over anyone. Instead, he uses it to help others, especially those who can't always stand up for themselves, like the Hobbits and the Dwarves of Erebor. He is also willing to listen to others, even when he knows more than they do. He lets Frodo decide which path the Fellowship takes, even when that means going through Moria. He is brave enough to sacrifice himself for his friends and never gives up on them. To me, Gandalf is a great example of what a leader should be.

  3. Treebeard

    Treebeard is the oldest living creature in Middle-earth,Tom Bombadil aside, and he's perfectly happy minding his own business and taking care of his forest. I love how he represents nature and a world that is slowly disappearing. He's peaceful, patient, and doesn't rush into anything. What I really like about him is that even though he's been around for thousands of years, he still takes the time to listen to Merry and Pippin, two Hobbits/creatures he had never even heard of. Eventually, he realizes that he can't just sit back while Saruman destroys everything around him. He rises and leads the Ents into battle, knowing it could be their last march. Treebeard reminds me that being strong doesn't mean you have to be loud or aggressive. Sometimes it means being patient and peaceful but knowing when it's time to stand up for something.

  4. Aragorn

    Aragorn, or Strider as we first know him, comes from a long line of kings and is the rightful heir to the throne of Gondor. But what I like about him is that he never seems interested in power or being king. He understands how much responsibility comes with that title and even fears that responsibility. He spends much of his life living in the wilderness as a Ranger, protecting others without expecting anything in return. He is a fearless warrior who never backs down from fighting darkness, but he is also humble and willing to put his life on the line for his friends. He turns down the temptation of the Ring. Eventually, he accepts who he is and becomes the king Middle-earth needs. I like to think Aragorn is proof that you can be a late bloomer in life and still go on to accomplish great things. He is proof that you are on your own timeline in this life and that you don't have to rush to be who you are meant to be.

  5. Gimli

    Gimli comes from a proud and strong lineage of Dwarves as well as one of the bravest members of the Fellowship. He's stubborn, quick tempered, and always ready to reach for his axe when there's a fight. But underneath all that, he's incredibly loyal to his friends. What I admire most about Gimli is how much he changes throughout the story. He starts out with a deep distrust of Elves, but eventually develops an incredible friendship with Legolas. Even during the darkest moments of their journey, he never abandons his companions. Gimli reminds me that no matter how stubborn or set in our ways we might be, we can always learn, grow, and become better people.

I think true greatness isn't about power, status, or recognition. It is in the small everyday acts and choices we make. In the words of Gandalf, "All we have to decide is what to do with the time that is given us."
